# Office Hours Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines the project's open support sessions: the schedule, the format, who
hosts, and how topics are submitted. Some questions are easier to answer in conversation
than in an issue thread, and a recurring, announced session gives newcomers a low-pressure
place to ask them. Without a stated format, office hours become an unstructured call
where whoever talks most sets the agenda.

## Scope

This policy covers open office hours for contributors and community members. It does not
cover:

- private support requests, which follow the [Support Policy](../policies/SUPPORT.md);
- security reports, which always use the private channel in the
  [Security Policy](../SECURITY_POLICY.md) and are never discussed in an open session;
- conduct concerns, which follow the
  [Code of Conduct Reporting](../COC_REPORTING.md) process; and
- mentor and mentee pairs, whose time is arranged separately under the
  [Mentorship Program](MENTORSHIP_PROGRAM.md).

## Schedule and Format

| Element | Arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| Frequency | **Fortnightly**, at minimum |
| Day and time | Fixed and published, chosen to avoid the project's busiest overlap window rather than a maintainer's convenience |
| Time zone | Published explicitly, with the session length set so it is workable across at least two regions |
| Length | **60 minutes**, with the last 10 reserved for open questions |
| Public or private | Public by default, announced in advance, with any recording made public afterwards if the participants agree |
| Notes | Notes published in the repository after every session, so attendance is not required to benefit |
| Language | English, with a written summary that is usable by non-native speakers. Where interpretation is needed, it is arranged in advance rather than improvised |

The fortnightly cadence is a floor, not a ceiling. A session is skipped rather than
cancelled late, and a skip is announced in advance with the reason.

## Who Hosts

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Host | Runs the session, keeps it to time, ensures questions are answered or assigned. Normally a maintainer, reviewer, or the [Community Manager](../roles/COMMUNITY_MANAGER.md) |
| Co-host | Handles the notes, the chat, and the queue of questions. Rotating among volunteers so the load is not always the same people |
| Any attendee | May raise a topic. Hosting is not required to participate |

Rules that apply to hosting:

- **Hosting is voluntary and rotates.** Someone who cannot host says so; the session is
  rescheduled or given without a host as an informal discussion.
- **The host is not on duty.** Office hours are not a support channel with an on-call
  commitment, and a question the host cannot answer is assigned rather than guessed at.
- **The host moderates for time, not for content.** A substantive disagreement belongs in
  an issue or the [Conflict Resolution Process](../processes/CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md),
  not in a call.
- **The host follows the Code of Conduct** in the same way as any session, per the
  [Code of Conduct Enforcement](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md).

## How Topics Are Submitted

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Submit | A topic is posted as an issue labelled `office-hours`, or as a comment on the session announcement, before the session |
| 2. Triage | The host reviews submissions, groups related ones, and publishes an agenda |
| 3. Prioritise | Where there is more time than topics, order by: blockers for a new contributor, questions with broad answers, then specific deep dives |
| 4. Open floor | The last 10 minutes are for anything not on the agenda |
| 5. Carry over | A topic that cannot be covered becomes an issue, so it is not lost |

Rules for topics:

- **Anyone may submit**, including someone who has never contributed. No topic is
  declined because it seems too basic; a question asked by one person is asked by the
  next.
- **State what you need.** A topic that names the problem and what an answer looks like
  is answerable. "I am stuck" is not, and the host will ask.
- **No personal data in a topic.** Security issues, conduct concerns, and anything
  containing personal data go to the private channels above, and the submitter is told
  where.
- **A topic is not a support ticket.** Anything needing sustained work is turned into an
  issue so it is tracked.

## Output

Each session produces three things:

1. **Notes**, published in the repository: what was discussed, what was decided, and what
   was left open. The notes are written so that someone who was not present can act on
   them.
2. **Follow-up issues** for anything that needs tracking, with the session referenced.
3. **Pointers**, where the answer is already written — a documentation link is a better
   answer than a verbal one, and a question answered by improving the documentation
   produces a lasting fix.

A session that produces notes nobody can use has not met the standard this policy sets.

## Accessibility

The session is designed to be attendable, because a format that excludes people is not
open:

- The agenda is published in advance, so someone can prepare or decide the topic is not
  for them.
- Notes are published afterwards, so attendance is never a requirement for receiving the
  information.
- Audio-only attendance is fully supported; no question requires being on video.
- Written questions are treated identically to spoken ones.
- Where a session cannot accommodate someone's needs — a sign-language interpreter, a
  translated summary — that is arranged in advance on request, without the requester
  having to justify it.

## Relationship to Other Support

Office hours complement rather than replace the issue tracker. Anything that needs a
tracked answer becomes an issue, so that it has a
[response expectation](../policies/SUPPORT.md) and a visible lifecycle. A question
answered in office hours and never written down is a question the next person asks
again, which is why the notes are mandatory.

## Enforcement Status

- Office hours are a human arrangement. There is no automation for reminders, agendas, or
  notes.
- Attendance and topic counts are gathered from the session issues themselves; no
  participant tracking beyond the session announcement is required.
- The Community Manager reviews the format every 12 months, and after any session where
  someone was unable to take part because of how it was run.
