# Ambassador Program

## Purpose

This policy defines who may speak for TeachLink Mobile, what an ambassador is expected
to do, how one is selected and recognised, and — just as importantly — what the program
does not authorise. Ambassadors are not a marketing tier; they are people trusted to
represent the project in public, which is a responsibility that needs limits as much as
it needs enthusiasm.

## Scope

This policy covers the ambassador role for community members who publicly represent
TeachLink Mobile. It covers responsibilities, selection, support provided by the
project, recognition, and the limits of what an ambassador may say or commit to.

It does not cover:

- official press statements, which follow the
  [Press and Media Policy](MEDIA_POLICY.md);
- governance representation, which follows the
  [Representation and Speaking Policy](REPRESENTATION.md);
- employment, contracting, or any compensated arrangement, which is not offered through
  this program; or
- moderation authority, which follows the [Moderator Role](../roles/MODERATOR.md) and is
  not conferred by being an ambassador.

## Responsibilities

An ambassador is a community member who has chosen to be a visible point of contact.
What that means in practice:

| Responsibility | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Answer questions | Help people find the answer in the documentation, and say so when the answer is not known |
| Represent accurately | Describe the project as it is, including its limits and its maturity |
| Set expectations | Point contributors at the [Contributor Onboarding Process](../processes/ONBOARDING.md) and [Support Policy](../policies/SUPPORT.md) rather than promising timelines |
| Point at governance | When someone asks how a decision is made, point at the [Charter](../CHARTER.md) and the governance folder |
| Model the culture | Follow the [Code of Conduct Enforcement](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md) and the [Inclusive Language Guide](INCLUSIVE_LANGUAGE.md) |
| Signpost support | Direct security concerns to the private channel in the [Security Policy](../SECURITY_POLICY.md), never to a public thread |
| Hand over early | Say "that is a maintainer's call" when something is not theirs to decide |

An ambassador is expected to be present and helpful, not to be always available. Time
given to the program is voluntary and unpaid.

## Selection

Ambassadors are nominated and confirmed, not self-appointed, and the process is
deliberately lightweight.

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Nomination | A contributor who has been active for at least 90 days is nominated, by a maintainer or by the Community Manager, with the reason |
| Evidence | The nomination cites specific contributions — issues triaged, questions answered, issues opened that turned out to be useful, documentation improvements |
| Confirmation | The [Community Manager](../roles/COMMUNITY_MANAGER.md) confirms the nomination after checking the [Code of Conduct](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md) record and that the person is still active |
| Announcement | The appointment is recorded, and the person is asked whether they want it listed publicly, since some people prefer the role quietly |

Selection is based on demonstrated helpfulness, not on reach, follower count, or
platform popularity. A person with a small following who answers accurately is a better
ambassador than a large one who confabulates.

The bar is stated so it can be applied consistently: someone who has been around long
enough to know where things are, has been helpful in public, and follows the code of
conduct.

## What Ambassadors May and May Not Do

This section exists because the most common problems with programs like this come from
vague authority.

| May | May not |
| --- | --- |
| Answer questions from the documentation and the repository | Invent an answer, or guess at a roadmap |
| Say what the project currently does | Promise a feature, a date, or a release |
| Confirm publicly available information | Disclose anything non-public, including security issues and unmerged work |
| Point to the governance documents | Speak on behalf of the project in a personal dispute |
| Decline and refer to a maintainer | Commit maintainer or project resources, including support time |
| Say "I do not know, let me find someone" | Present a personal opinion as the project's position |
| Escalate a concern to the Community Manager | Handle a conduct report, which follows the [Code of Conduct Reporting](../COC_REPORTING.md) process |

An ambassador who exceeds these limits has the title withdrawn by the Community Manager.
Withdrawal is a decision under the [Privilege Revocation Policy](../policies/REVOCATION.md)
and is recorded.

## Support Provided by the Project

Being an ambassador should not mean absorbing cost and frustration personally. The
project provides:

- a way to ask maintainers for an answer on something a user is waiting for;
- advance notice of releases, deprecations, and breaking changes, so an ambassador is
  not surprised by an announcement;
- a private channel with the Community Manager for questions about what may be said; and
- removal from the role on request, at any time and without a reason.

What an ambassador does **not** receive: a stipend, equipment, travel, or priority
access to maintainers. Nothing in this program is a paid arrangement, and an ambassador
who incurs cost does so on the understanding that it is voluntary.

## Recognition

Ambassadors are credited under the
[Contributor Recognition and Credits Policy](../RECOGNITION.md):

- listed in the project credits, at the ambassador's choice of visibility;
- credited in release notes for significant community work, following the
  [Changelog Policy](../policies/CHANGELOG_POLICY.md);
- thanked publicly by the maintainers when their help resolves a user's problem, and
  eligible for nomination for a role under the
  [Role Nomination Process](../processes/NOMINATION.md) on the strength of their
  community contribution, though ambassador status is not by itself a qualification; and
- named as an ambassador in the project documentation, if they want to be.

Recognition is proportionate to the work and does not imply a status the person has not
earned elsewhere. An ambassador who wants a maintainer role is judged on the
[Role Promotion Criteria](../processes/PROMOTION_CRITERIA.md), as anyone else is.

## Review and End of Role

- The program list is reviewed **every 12 months** by the Community Manager.
- An ambassador who is inactive, or whose activity no longer meets the expectations, is
  discussed with them before anything is changed. They may simply prefer to step down.
- An ambassador may leave at any time, with no reason required and no exit interview.
- A person whose conduct raises a concern is handled under the
  [Code of Conduct Enforcement](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md) first. The ambassador title is
  never the subject of the decision, and a genuine good-faith report about an ambassador
  is handled through the reporting process, not by quietly removing the title.

## Enforcement Status

- The program is a human arrangement with no automated component. The list is maintained
  by the Community Manager.
- [Code of Conduct](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md) enforcement and moderation authority are
  separate systems and are not implied by ambassador status.
- The boundaries in "What Ambassadors May and May Not Do" are enforced by conversation
  and, where necessary, by withdrawal of the title.
