# Representation and Speaking Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines who may represent TeachLink Mobile in public, such as at
conferences, meetups, webinars, and panels, how that is approved, and what a
representative must disclose. It protects the project from being misrepresented, and
protects speakers from being unsure what they are allowed to say on its behalf.

## Scope

This policy covers talks, workshops, panels, demos, and similar appearances in which a
person speaks as, or is introduced as, part of TeachLink Mobile. It applies to
maintainers, role holders, and contributors.

It does not restrict a person from speaking about their own work in a personal
capacity. Contacts from journalists and statements to the press are covered by the
[Press and Media Policy](MEDIA_POLICY.md).

## Who May Represent the Project

| Person | May represent the project? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any contributor | No, but may say they contribute | May describe their own contributions accurately. Has no authority to speak for the project |
| Reviewer, Triager, or Committer | No, unless designated | May state their role. Speaking for the project requires designation |
| Maintainer | Yes, for technical topics, after notifying the Community Manager | Does not speak to governance disputes, conduct matters, or security issues under embargo |
| [Community Manager](../roles/COMMUNITY_MANAGER.md) | Yes | The default official representative |
| A designated speaker | Yes, for the named event and topic only | Designation is written and expires after the event |

"Represent the project" means speaking as, or being introduced as, a voice of
TeachLink Mobile, as opposed to describing a personal experience of it.

## Approval Process

1. **Request.** The prospective speaker opens an issue describing the event, the
   audience, the topic, the date, and whether the project is being asked to pay any
   cost.
2. **Review.** The Community Manager and one Maintainer review the request within
   **seven days**. They check that the topic is within what the speaker can represent,
   that the speaker has no unmanaged conflict, and that the event is consistent with
   the project's values and [Code of Conduct](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md).
3. **Materials.** Slides, demos, and handouts are shared with the Community Manager at
   least **three business days** before the event. They are checked for correct use of
   the name and logo under the [Trademark Policy](../policies/TRADEMARK.md), and for
   confidential information.
4. **Decision.** The reviewers approve, approve with conditions, or decline, and record
   the decision and reasons in the issue.
5. **After the event.** The speaker posts a short summary in the issue, including any
   commitments or promises made on the project's behalf, which the Community Manager
   follows up.

A request that arrives with less notice than this allows can still be approved by the
Community Manager and a Maintainer; the checks are not skipped.

## What a Representative May and May Not Say

A representative:

- describes the released app, published governance, and the public roadmap accurately;
- may share opinions, if clearly marked as their own;
- does not make commitments, announce dates, or promise features that are not in the
  published roadmap;
- does not speak about unreleased security issues, user data, private conduct matters,
  or other confidential information; and
- does not imply endorsement by, or a partnership with, any organisation without
  written agreement.

If asked something outside what they can speak to, a representative says they will
follow up, and refers the question to the Community Manager.

## Disclosure Rules

A representative discloses, at the start of the appearance or in the materials:

- **Affiliation.** Their relationship to the project and to any employer or client with
  an interest in the topic.
- **Funding.** If the event or the speaker is funded by an organisation with an
  interest in the project, who paid and what they paid for, including travel and
  accommodation that was covered.
- **Conflicts.** Any personal or commercial interest in a product or service they
  recommend.
- **Capacity.** Whether they speak for the project or for themselves. A personal
  opinion is labelled as such.

A representative who finds, before or after an event, that a disclosure was incomplete
corrects it promptly and tells the Community Manager.

## Conduct and Revocation

Representatives are bound by the project's conduct expectations at events and online.
The Community Manager and Maintainers may withdraw a designation, with a stated reason,
if a representative misstates the project, breaks these rules, or is subject to an
enforcement action under the [conduct enforcement policy](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md). A
withdrawn designation ends the person's authority to represent the project; their
personal speech is not restricted by this policy.
